# Theoretical Foundations Audit — First-Principles Analysis of the Recurrent Framework

Date: 2026-07-31. Strategy lane, for Mark's review. Companion to PAPER_TWO_PHASE2_PROGRAM_DECISION_AND_DESIGN_20260731.md (draft 2). Purpose: ground the phase-2 designs in explicit mathematics before experimental spend; identify first-principles errors in past and proposed designs; produce checkable predictions. Notation follows Mark's sidecar design note: base model split as prelude/block/coda with hidden width d = 896; h₀ the pretrained activation at the insertion point; the sidecar update h_{k+1} = h₀ + ρ_k(h_k − h₀) + γ_k Δh_k with bounded writeback ‖Δh_k‖ ≤ M = c·RMS(h₀)·√d.

## 1. The three mechanisms as dynamical systems

**In-place vertical looping** is the iteration h_{k+1} = F(h_k) where F = block is a pretrained residual map, F(h) = h + f(h), Jacobian J_F = I + J_f. F was optimized for exactly one application inside a feedforward pass; nothing in pretraining constrains the spectrum of J_f, so F is not a contraction and has no attracting task-relevant fixed point. Iterating it moves states off the manifold 𝓜₁ = image of one application — the distribution the coda was trained to read. The drift ‖F^k(h) − h‖ is generically unbounded in k for non-contractive F, and the coda's error grows with the input distribution shift. Predicted phenomenology: harm that appears at the first extra iterate and persists or grows with depth, on any checkpoint of this lineage, trained or not. This is the parity ledger (net −6,625 pre-D0, −5,325 post-D0 at 1→2; −7,623/−7,803 at 2→3) and the EMA audit's perturbation-compounding result. **Retrodiction 1: dynamical systems predicted the in-place harm asymmetry before any experiment ran.** The looped-transformer literature is consistent, not contradictory: weight-tied loops are expressive (they can emulate iterative algorithms) *when trained as loops*; the failure is retrofitting iteration onto a map trained as a function. The composition thesis survives; the retrofit-without-stabilization method does not.

**Transient append** replaces the emission pathway: the token is read at a new position whose input distribution (fed hidden state at raw scale) was never seen in training. There is no drift accumulation — each episode is one step — but there is also no fallback: the readout at the slot *is* the answer. Formally the mechanism computes emit(G(h)) where G is the full stack applied to an out-of-distribution input, and safety requires G to approximate the identity on the emission-relevant quotient for ~74 percent of positions while transforming the rest. That is a conditional function (section 3), and the linear bridge could not represent it.

**The anchored sidecar** has, by construction, the stability the other two lack. Let e_k = h_k − h₀. Then e_{k+1} = ρ e_k + γΔ_k with ‖Δ_k‖ ≤ M, so

  ‖e_k‖ ≤ γM(1 − ρ^k)/(1 − ρ) ≤ γM/(1 − ρ).

The loop trajectory lives in a tube of radius γM/(1−ρ) around the pretrained state, **uniformly in loop count K**. With the version-one initializations (ρ ≈ 0.8, γ ≈ 0.05, c ≈ 0.02) the tube radius is ≈ 0.25·cM-scale — a small, controlled neighborhood of 𝓜₁. The coda's input is therefore in-distribution at every loop; the in-place failure mode is structurally excluded. Backward direction: ∂e_K/∂e_k = ρ^{K−k}·∏(I + γ·∂Δ/∂e) ≈ ρ^{K−k}(1 + O(γ)) — no explosion (ρ < 1, γ small) and controlled attenuation (ρ³ ≈ 0.51 at ρ = 0.8, K = 4). Loop-state mixing strengthens this to a guarantee: with h̄_k = Σ a_{k,i}h_i, every loop state has a gradient path of length one to the final loss with weight a_{K,i}; vanishing is impossible at any K so long as the mixture weights stay bounded away from zero. *Proviso:* the same γ/(1−ρ) that guarantees stability is the expressivity budget (section 2), and if training anneals ρ → 1 while growing γ, the tube dissolves and we recover unanchored drift. **Design amendment A1: cap ρ ≤ 0.9 in version one and log the realized tube radius max_k‖h_k − h₀‖/RMS(h₀) as an atlas quantity.**

*In plain terms: rethinking-in-place walks away from home and gets lost; the scratchpad-with-anchor is on a leash whose length we set. The leash is why it can't get lost — and also why it can only reach so far. That reach is the next section.*

## 2. The expressivity bound, and the one check that must precede the window (V1)

The bounded correction can only change what it can reach. A correction of norm ‖Δh̃‖ at the insertion point moves the final logits by at most Lip(F_{>L})·‖Δh̃‖ in logit space, where Lip(F_{>L}) is the local Lipschitz gain of the upper stack. To flip the emitted token from a to b requires crossing the logit margin m_{ab}. So the sidecar can fix exactly those errors whose margin satisfies

  m_{ab} ≤ Lip(F_{>L}) · γM/(1 − ρ).

Neither factor is currently known: Lip(F_{>L}) is measurable by JVP probes on dev states (forward-only), and the margin distribution of the *oracle-help* positions — the 7,799 positions a perfect referee banks — is computable from one cheap dev pass. **V1, the go/no-go-shaping computation: overlay the reachable-Δlogit bound on the margin distribution of oracle helps.** Prediction, stated in advance: helps concentrate at small margins (the fragility data already shows hurts cluster in the teacher's mid-confidence quartiles, where margins are thin), so the bound clears most of them at c ≈ 0.02–0.05. If instead the helps sit at large margins, the bounded-correction premise caps out low regardless of training quality — and the remedy is known in advance (raise c, or the E4 upper-layer LoRA, which raises the effective Lipschitz gain along trained directions). Either way, E4 acquires its trigger condition from theory rather than frustration.

## 3. Why the linear bridge had to fail: the conditional-function argument (Retrodiction 2)

Safety plus utility is a conditional specification: on the preserve-set A (baseline-correct positions, ≈ 74 percent) the mechanism must act as the identity on the emission-relevant quotient; on the improve-set B it must apply position-specific corrections. A and B are not geometrically segregated — both are generic hidden states drawn from overlapping distributions, so span(A) ≈ ℝᵈ. A linear correction ΔW·h that is uniformly small on span(A) is small everywhere, including on B. Therefore the constrained optimum of a linear bridge is: suppress damage as far as possible, gain few new helps — **which is precisely Stage A's measured outcome (89.8 percent of improvement from prevented harms, helps up only 21 percent, band missed).** The failure was computable a priori. Conditionality requires the minimal nonlinearity that can represent "if": a gate g(φ(h))·correction, which is representable exactly when A and B are separable in the gate's feature space φ. This is not a detail; it is the theorem-shaped statement the whole phase turns on:

> **The reduction.** Every phase-2 mechanism succeeds or fails on one measurable quantity: the separability of improvable from fragile positions in accessible features. The gate consumes separability (it can only be as selective as φ permits); the anchor protects the state so separability is not destroyed in transit; the probes measure it. A Fano-type bound makes this quantitative: no gate on features φ can exceed the utility implied by the probe's discrimination on φ. The probe battery is therefore the ceiling-measurement for the entire design, and probes-first ordering is a theoretical requirement, not a scheduling preference.

The same argument governs D1: the control-token gate is a conditional over the same feature space, read at a different point. Rung 0's negative bounded *post-hoc scalar* features only; the trained pathway reads the full hidden state, a strictly richer φ. The T1 precedent (frozen pooled head 9.1 percent versus trained pathway 100 percent on the same substrate) is the empirical form of that strictness. D1's bet, stated theoretically: separability exists in the full state that scalar summaries destroy. The probe battery tests this directly by comparing probe AUC from scalar features against probe AUC from full states — **the same computation adjudicates both lines' priors before either trains.**

## 4. Objectives: descent, conflict, and collapse

**The margin-improvement loss is a Lyapunov condition.** Requiring q_{k+1,j} ≥ q_k,j + m (with stop-gradient on the earlier loop) asks the loop to be a descent method on teacher log-loss — a monotone value function along the trajectory. This is the correct shape for trainable iteration, with one flaw: monotone improvement is infeasible on positions already at ceiling or beyond reach, and penalizing the infeasible injects noise. **Design amendment A2: mask the improvement loss to positions not already correct at loop k (or teacher-rejected positions), so the Lyapunov constraint is only imposed where descent is possible.**

**Gradient conflict is the predictable pathology of five losses on shared small parameters.** The null-loop loss and the CE loss push the shared gates in opposite directions on A versus B, and their conflict is resolvable only through the gate features — the reduction again. Magnitude balancing (the design's EMA-adjusted weights) fixes scale but not direction: two objectives with negative gradient cosine oscillate at any weighting. **Design amendment A3: add inter-objective gradient cosines (per module) to the gradient atlas**, with the pre-stated reading that persistent cosine < 0 between the token loss and the feature/null losses on bridge parameters indicates the feature set cannot serve both masters, and the remedy is loss scheduling (alternation) before any architecture change.

**Collapse has a metric.** The null-loop loss at excessive weight drives γ → 0 globally (the coward solution the anti-degeneracy floor catches at evaluation — but evaluation is too late). The scratchpad has its own collapse mode: slots converging to a single direction, reducing effective capacity from M slots to one. **Design amendment A4: log scratchpad effective rank (participation ratio of slot covariance) and the open-rate of every gate as atlas quantities, with collapse thresholds pre-stated at window opening.**

**The feature-anchoring loss is theoretically necessary on this substrate, in the exact form proposed.** Our own scale results showed norm carries load-bearing information here (raw-scale transmission, RMS-matching collapse). A cosine-only distillation discards norm; the design's log-scale term retains it. The stop-gradient teacher prevents the degenerate solution in which the target migrates toward the predictor. Both choices are validated by receipts we already own.

**The supervision weights match the information structure.** Conditional entropy H(y_{t+j} | x_{≤t}) is non-decreasing in j, so geometrically declining horizon weights v_j track declining achievable signal; increasing loop weights w_k allocate credit toward the states that emit. And the RTG construction for D1 is supervised adaptive computation — ACT's ponder cost with measured labels in place of REINFORCE's high-variance halting gradient, which is the theoretically sounder estimator by a wide margin.

## 5. Sample complexity and precision (Retrodiction 3, and standing constraints)

Stage A's objective sampled one position per step at batch 1: gradient variance ∝ 1/(batch × positions-per-row), and 83 percent of logged gradients at the clip means the realized update direction was frequently the clipped direction, not the gradient direction. The information-rate principle (dense supervision, real batches) is the direct fix and is already standing policy; the theory adds only the quantitative form: to resolve an effect of relative size ε in u, the number of independent supervised positions must scale like 1/ε² — at ε ≈ 0.001 (the qualification CI floor), millions of position-visits, which dense supervision delivers in a few epochs and single-position sampling never approaches. RG-11's fp32 requirement is the numerical face of the same mathematics: the loop's long Jacobian products lose the low-order bits that carry exactly the small-correction signals the sidecar trades in; bf16's 8-bit mantissa is below the tube radius in relative terms. Full fp32 stays non-negotiable for every composite gradient.

## 6. Scorecard: is the program grounded enough to be worth the spend?

**What is now grounded.** The preserve-plus-bounded-correction invariant (residual-learning theory: zero-initialized identity-anchored branches are trainable without destroying the carrier — ReZero/LayerScale logic, plus the section 1 contraction bound). Deep supervision and loop mixing (short gradient paths; no vanishing at any K). The feature loss form (norm-preserving, stop-gradient — validated by our own scale receipts). The halting recipe (supervised ACT). The precision and information-rate policies (section 5).

**What was never grounded, and what it cost.** Iterating a non-contraction (predicted the in-place harm ledger — two registered rounds spent measuring it). A linear map for a conditional task (predicted Stage A's exact failure shape). Starved supervision (predicted Stage A's noise floor). Three retrodictions, three registered negatives. The purpose of this audit is that the *next* failure, if it comes, is predicted in advance instead: the candidate is the expressivity-margin bound, and V1 checks it before the window spends anything.

**Verification computations, ordered, all cheap, all before or at window opening:**

- **V1 — expressivity check (pre-window, the only one that shapes go/no-go).** Margin distribution of oracle-help positions from dev passes, overlaid with the reachable-Δlogit bound Lip(F_{>L})·γM/(1−ρ), Lipschitz measured by JVP on dev states. Prediction: most helps reachable at c ≈ 0.02–0.05. Failure mode and remedy pre-stated in section 2.
- **V2 — block iteration gain (pre-window, forward-only).** JVP gain distribution of the pretrained block at 1–4 iterates on dev states: quantifies the non-contraction account of the in-place ledger, and gives the manuscript its mechanism sentence for the harm asymmetry.
- **V3 — separability ceiling (E1, already planned as the probe battery; now with theoretical status).** Probe AUC from full states versus scalar features: the Fano-style ceiling for the DC2 gate *and* the adjudication of D1's prior, in one computation.
- **V4 — conflict cosines (E1 onward; atlas addition A3).**
- **V5 — collapse metrics (E1 onward; atlas addition A4: effective rank, open rates, tube radius per A1).**

**Verdict.** With amendments A1–A4 adopted and V1–V2 run before the window opens, the phase-2 program is grounded: every structural choice either carries a stability or expressivity argument traceable to a bound, or is explicitly the measurement of the one quantity (separability) the bounds cannot supply from theory alone. The honest residual risks are quantified rather than hidden: V1 may reveal the correction budget is too small (remedy pre-stated), and V3 may reveal the separability ceiling is low — in which case *both* lines are bounded by the same wall, that wall is a publishable finding about where arbitration information lives, and we will have found it for the price of forward passes rather than training runs.

*In plain terms: the mathematics says the new design cannot fail the way the old ones failed — the anchor forbids getting lost, the gate can represent "leave it alone," and every loop gets its own teaching signal. What the mathematics cannot promise is that the model's states contain enough information to tell "fixable" from "fragile," or that the leash is long enough to reach the fixes. Those two are exactly what the pre-window checks measure, for pennies, before any training money is spent.*
