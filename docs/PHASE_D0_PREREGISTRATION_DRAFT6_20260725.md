## 1. Registered question

On natural text, what fraction of a larger same-family teacher's corrections to the small recurrent drafter is recoverable by shallow adaptive vertical depth — one to four loops — gated by the trained control-token pathway?

### 1.1 Depth-equivalence context (back-of-envelope, descriptive, added markup round 3)

With the surgery's structure (6 prelude layers, 12 looped, 6 coda), effective sequential depth at L loops is 12 plus 12L layers. Matching raw layer counts across the Qwen2.5 family: the 7B (28 layers) is matched at 1.33 loops, the 14B (48 layers) at exactly 3, the 32B (64 layers) at 4.33, and the 72B (80 layers) at 5.67. The 1-to-4 loop budget therefore spans the raw sequential depth of the family through 32B. Compute-matching tells the opposite story: iso-FLOPs with the 7B requires roughly 35 loops and with the 14B roughly 73, and across the ladder from 0.5B to 72B, depth grows 3.3x while per-layer width compute grows 59x and non-embedding parameters grow 196x. The family buys capability overwhelmingly in width. (Per-layer accounting validates against receipts: it reproduces the recurrent block at 178.9M versus the receipted 180.6M forward-active, the difference being norms and biases. Family specs are from the Qwen2.5 technical report and are verified against local checkpoint configs at lock.)

Working hypothesis this phase informs, stated as interpretation and not as a registered claim: width predominantly buys knowledge storage (parameters scale with width squared) while depth buys composition — more complex transformation of the latent state — and the weight-tied loop is a clean instrument because each loop adds composition at zero added storage. Under that framing R estimates the composition share of the small-to-large gap. One sharp interpretive signature follows from the arithmetic: at 2 loops the drafter already exceeds the 7B teacher's sequential depth, so under a pure-depth account the depth-response curve should flatten by about 2 loops against a 7B teacher. Continued gains at 3 and 4 loops would indicate tied-weight reuse inefficiency rather than depth shortage. The dual-teacher design turns this into a registered two-point test (markup round 5, 2026-07-25): the 14B sits at exactly 3 loops equivalent, so the pair brackets the 2-to-4 iteration question — under the pure-depth account the saturation point should shift from about 2 loops against the 7B to about 3 against the 14B. Section 7 carries the pre-stated readings. Neither axis is expected to be pure. The composite extension of this accounting — how horizontal Coconut-style steps multiply with vertical loops to reach teacher-class compute budgets that neither axis reaches alone — lives in the concept note, section 5.2. D0 stays vertical-only.

This is registered as a measurement, not a bet. The depth-recoverable fraction is a publishable characterization of the small-to-large capability gap whichever way it comes out, because rejections split into compute-limited errors that more depth can fix and knowledge-limited errors that no depth can fix, and no one has measured that split on a loopable substrate. Secondary question, with independent systems value: does adaptive depth raise the drafter's acceptance rate against its teacher at acceptable expected-loop cost?

## 3. Labeling protocol

- Corpus (revised 2026-07-24 per markup; dump policy resolved 2026-07-25): two fixed, hashed strata totaling 2M tokens. (a) General text: a FineWeb-Edu slice drawn from a 2025-dated Common Crawl dump — the exact dump ID and all partition hashes are fixed by the coding agent at lock and recorded in preregistration.json — so neither drafter nor teacher has seen it. Post-cutoff provenance keeps memorized text from inflating agreement and keeps "recovered by depth" from conflating computation with differential recall. The teacher is also ignorant of post-cutoff facts, which is acceptable because the registered target is teacher agreement, not truth. (b) Code: a permissively licensed slice of The Stack v2 deduplicated, license metadata retained — the stratum where prediction is most compute-like and the depth-recoverable fraction is plausibly highest. Document-level split into label-train, calibration, and evaluation partitions within each stratum, roughly 1.6M, 0.2M, and 0.2M tokens pooled. No document appears in two partitions. A 100k-token in-pretraining-era contrast slice is labeled descriptively to measure how memorization shifts acceptance rates.
- Corpus density probe (pre-lock, forward-only, uncitable): 100k tokens per candidate slice, measuring rejection density — disagreements per thousand tokens, which is labels per labeling dollar. The final strata composition (default 50/50 general/code) is chosen by density and domain coverage and filed with the probe receipts before lock.
- No think-token or reasoning-trace corpora in D0. Trace supervision is the C-track's horizontal axis on the Qwen3 substrate. The D0 drafter carries no think tokens, trace-formatted text is out-of-distribution formatting for it, and mixing the corpora would conflate the program's two supervision axes.
- Single-pass teacher caching contract (markup round 6, 2026-07-25). Each teacher runs exactly one forward pass per partition it labels, emitting a compact per-position record: teacher greedy token id, drafter-token logprob and rank under the teacher, teacher entropy, and the KL against the plain drafter streamed within the same pass. All downstream measurement compares against cached records — no teacher reloads after its labeling pass. The 14B's total footprint is therefore one forward-only pass over the 0.2M-token calibration partition. Cached records are hashed with the partitions.
- Context: teacher-forced corpus context at every position (both models see the true prefix). This gives dense per-position labels without compounding drift, at the cost of being off-policy relative to free-running drafting — accepted for a pilot, noted in do-not-claim.
- Primary label, exact-match rule: a position is accepted if the drafter's greedy token equals the teacher's greedy token, rejected otherwise. Deterministic, binary, receipts-friendly.
- Recorded per position regardless of label (the full disagreement-signal vector, per the concept-note markup): the drafter token's rank under the teacher, the drafter token's probability under the teacher, the per-position KL from teacher to drafter, teacher entropy, and the length of the surrounding rejection run. Stochastic-acceptance metrics are computed descriptively from these; the exact-match rule alone drives labels.

## 4. Calibration procedure (locked), with the pre-stated fallback

The disagreement-to-depth mapping is calibrated, not assumed.

Step one, floor measurement. Before any D0 training, run the drafter at forced depths 1 through 6 on the calibration partition's rejected positions and record per-loop agreement-with-teacher and KL-to-teacher, binned by KL quartile of the disagreement signal, against both calibration teachers. This is the pre-training depth-response curve. Measurement extends to depth 6 (markup round 5) so both teachers' saturation points are observable inside the window — training targets remain capped at 4. The same 1-through-6 calibration sweep repeats on the trained model for the section 7 teacher-shift test.

Step two, branch — stated in advance:

- If the floor curve is graded (depth-4 agreement exceeds depth-1 agreement by at least 2 points in at least two severity bins), fit the mapping from it: for each severity bin, the target depth is the smallest d whose agreement is within 1 point of that bin's depth-4 agreement, and bins failing the 2-point condition map to depth 1. Training then uses these binned targets.
- If the floor curve is flat — the expected case, since Paper One found untrained extra loops harm natural text — training uses the dynamic rule instead: the stop target at each position is the first loop whose output matches the teacher, capped at 4, and positions that never match by loop 4 receive a depth-4 target with distillation supervision. The calibrated disagreement-to-depth curve is then measured from the trained model on the calibration partition and filed as a receipted deliverable.

Either way the mapping's fitting procedure locks here, its values are outputs, and the curve — depth needed as a function of disagreement severity — is a primary deliverable of the phase.

Functional form of the mapping (added in markup round 2). The shape of the loop function is measured, not assumed. The primary fit is a monotone nonparametric (isotonic) regression of required depth on each divergence signal — monotonicity is the only imposed assumption, since more divergence never warrants less depth. Against the isotonic fit, three parametric candidates are compared on held-out calibration data: depth linear in rejection-run length (one loop per diverged token), depth linear in log KL, and a saturating form. Which family fits best is itself a receipted deliverable. Two structural notes. First, because labeling is teacher-forced, a rejection run is a span of sustained difficulty rather than a cascade of the drafter's own errors — every position is labeled with the true prefix in context — so run length enters the fit as a severity regressor in its own right, alongside per-position KL and rank. Second, every candidate form saturates at the loop budget of 4, which Paper One's off-task harm boundary fixes independently of any fit, so the fitted function is flat at and beyond the budget by construction. Positions inside rejection runs longer than 8 tokens are excluded from mapping calibration and reported as a descriptive tail, on the hypothesis that very long sustained divergence is knowledge-dominated (cap of 8 confirmed in markup round 4).

## 5. Training

- Loss: retained language-model cross-entropy on corpus tokens, plus a distillation KL to the teacher's distribution at the final executed loop of each rejected position, plus lambda times the control cross-entropy from the section-4 stop targets. Lambda 0.5 and equal class weights are carried as locked constants (resolved 2026-07-25) — they now hold two receipts, the P0 selection and the full T1-lite-R run in which the same constants produced exact selection at every trained depth. No mini-check.
- Rehearsal (resolved 2026-07-25): 30 percent synthetic mechanism rehearsal from the T1 family, aligned with the T1-lite-R receipted recipe rather than the earlier 25 percent proposal, protecting the installed actuator and mechanism.
- Budget: pilot scale — 4,000 steps, batch 1, AdamW with the T1-lite optimizer settings, seed 0, EMA 0.999, final-step EMA primary. Guardrail evaluations at steps 1,000, 2,000, and 3,000 on a held-out slice, descriptive with the T1-style abort rule (flat control loss and zero stop recall).

## 6. Primary readouts (evaluation partition, never touched before final evaluation)

1. Depth-recoverable fraction R: among rejected positions, the self-halted match rate within the 4-loop budget minus the loop-1 match rate. The headline number.
2. Acceptance-rate uplift: simulated greedy speculative decoding against the teacher, plain drafter versus adaptive drafter — acceptance rate, expected loops per token, and the implied compute-per-accepted-token, reported at draft windows gamma in {2, 4, 8} for comparability with the speculative-decoding literature (resolved 2026-07-25). The systems number. Wall-clock capture (markup round 7): tokens per second and per-position latency are measured for both drafters in the same simulation harness and banked in the receipts for future analysis — explicitly simulation-grade (Colab, small batch), never cited as an efficiency claim per section 8.
3. The calibrated depth-response curve by severity bin (from section 4), including the knowledge-versus-compute decomposition: the fraction of rejections unrecovered at depth 4, by bin.
4. Loop-usage distribution under self-halting: does the gate spend depth where the teacher disagrees (correlation of executed loops with the disagreement signals), or uniformly?
5. R and the depth-response curve reported per stratum (general text, code) as well as pooled — the per-domain split is part of the headline deliverable.
6. ARC allocation probe, descriptive only: run the trained self-halting drafter over ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge items and report whether executed depth correlates with item difficulty and concentrates at answer positions. No question-answering capability claim of any kind attaches to this probe. Forward intent, recorded not registered (markup round 7): D0 installs the mechanism — later phases plan capability evaluations on ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, and a coding benchmark, comparing the recursive drafter against its base model and teachers, under their own preregistrations.

Guardrails, both hard: on accepted positions, the loop-1 match rate must not fall more than 1 point below the pre-training drafter (natural-surface preservation), and the synthetic rehearsal check set must hold within 3 points of the T1-lite final checkpoint (mechanism retention).

## 7. Interpretation bands (stated in advance, descriptive — D0 has guardrails but no pass/fail gate)

- R below 2 points: depth is not recoverable through this recipe at this scale. The D-axis pauses; the C-track carries the program.
- R between 2 and 10 points: partial recoverability. D1 design proceeds only with an explicit account of which severity bins responded.
- R above 10 points: strong recoverability. D1 (scaled corpus, on-policy labeling, possible RLVR polish with acceptance as the exact reward, per the RLM precedent) becomes the next registered phase.

Bands bind interpretation language, not further work — what runs next is a WP4-style strategy decision either way.

Registered teacher-shift signature test (markup round 5, 2026-07-25; measurement design tightened in round 6 — a banded interpretive test, not a gate, consistent with this document's registration style). On the calibration partition, the trained drafter runs one forced-depth sweep at depths 1 through 6, and that single sweep is scored against both teachers' cached records simultaneously — the dual-teacher comparison adds zero drafter compute and reloads no teacher. Curves are agreement-rate curves (drafter greedy at depth d equals cached teacher greedy), with saturation defined as the smallest depth within 1 point of that curve's depth-6 value. Two complementary readouts: the per-teacher saturation comparison, and the stratified difference-set comparison — positions rejected by both teachers versus positions only the 14B rejects (the increment the deeper teacher adds), with the 7B-only set reported as a check. Under depth-as-composition the 14B-only increment should demand deeper saturation than the both-reject set. Pre-stated readings:

- Saturation shifts upward with teacher depth, from about 2 loops (7B) toward about 3 (14B): depth demand tracks the teacher gap, supporting the depth-as-composition account.
- Saturation is the same against both teachers: depth demand is a property of the text, not of the gap — the composition framing weakens and difficulty-intrinsic depth strengthens.
- Neither curve saturates by depth 6: budget-limited or reuse-inefficient, deliberately recorded as inconclusive between those two.

The reading is reported in the manuscript's interpretation language and binds no further work.

## 8. Do not claim

- That recovered agreement is reasoning — it is teacher-agreement on next-token prediction.
- Any general efficiency claim without wall-clock measurement on real speculative-decoding infrastructure.
- Generalization beyond the labeled corpus distribution, or on-policy drafting behavior (labels are teacher-forced).
- Anything about GRAM, stochastic width, or the closed Arm G route.
- Seed robustness (single seed) or scale robustness (one drafter, one teacher).
- That unrecovered rejections are knowledge-limited — the decomposition is by depth response, and "unrecovered at 4" does not distinguish deeper-recoverable from unrecoverable.
- Any question-answering or benchmark-capability claim from the ARC allocation probe.
