## 1. The Clifford-algebra angle (context for the coding agent — read before implementing TM-2g)

**The algebra in four sentences.** For vectors u, v the dot product u·v = |u||v|cosθ measures *alignment* and is a scalar. The wedge (exterior) product u∧v, with coefficients (u∧v)_{ij} = u_i v_j − u_j v_i for i<j, measures *oriented separation*: it names the 2-plane through which u would rotate to reach v, with a sign for direction and magnitude |u||v||sinθ|. The geometric (Clifford) product combines them, uv = u·v + u∧v, and is nothing more than the symmetric/antisymmetric decomposition of a product: u·v = (uv+vu)/2, u∧v = (uv−vu)/2. Equivalently, u∧v is the skew-symmetric matrix uvᵀ − vuᵀ, which has D(D−1)/2 independent coefficients — too many to compute densely at our widths, hence the sketching below.

**Two facts that govern every design decision in TM-2g:**

1. **The wedge's *magnitude* is redundant.** |u∧v|² = |u|²|v|² − (u·v)² — sin² from cos². If you know the dot product and both norms, the wedge norm adds nothing. All new information is in the **signed plane components** — *which* plane, *which* orientation. Never build a statistic on |wedge| alone.
2. **In high dimensions, everything looks rotational.** A generic direction has |cos| ~ 1/√d against any fixed state, so "the displacement is mostly perpendicular" is the default condition of random vectors, not a finding. Raw rotational fractions are quoted only for calibration; **every substantive claim is comparative** — success strata versus the D_none control stratum, within battery, per the r2 battery-stratification law.

**Why you must not apply this in the raw residual basis.** The published CliffordNet trick samples wedge coefficients along channel-shift diagonals (u_i v_{i+s} − u_{i+s} v_i), which presumes channel i is meaningfully "next to" channel i+s. Our banked gauge finding (CKA 0.0715 between seeds — coordinates as unrelated as chance allows) says the residual basis carries no canonical channel structure at all: raw-basis diagonals would sample planes of an arbitrary coordinate system. All TM-2g geometry is therefore computed in the **whitened stitched student frame** (whitening per r2: RMT-shrunk covariance on the broad pool — closed-form, D5-clean), with any dimensionality reduction done by a **frozen, seeded random projection** — never a learned projector, which would be training. (Two engineering caveats from the source paper, for the record: its linear-complexity claim ignores the dense O(|S|D²) re-projection in the released code, and its evidence is CIFAR-100-scale. We are borrowing the *lens*, not the architecture. Refs: arXiv 2601.06793; github.com/ParaMind2025/CAN.)

**The program translation (why this read earns its place).** In this language, every banked failure of this program was a *symmetric-part* phenomenon: the collapsed recurrent loop's margins followed m_{k+1} = r·m_k + c — contraction plus constant drift, an input-forgetting flow with no state-conditional rotation; the fixed-direction injections of W1 were state-independent pushes; W2′'s hemisphere-difference channel carried 62.5% of fit energy and predicted nothing. The live hypothesis TM-2g tests is the antisymmetric complement: **successful computation (the teachers', on rows they solve and the student fails) turns through consistent latent planes; unsuccessful computation does not.** If true, it simultaneously motivates the deferred Clifford mechanisms in §5 and specifies what any future trained module should learn. If false, the lens is banked as a redescription with no discriminative content, cheaply.
