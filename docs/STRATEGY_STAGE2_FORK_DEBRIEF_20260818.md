# Strategy — Fork Debrief: Formal Analysis, Creative Alternatives, and Literature for the Floor-Scale Campaign Decision

Date: 2026-08-18. Requested by Mark: pros and cons of the three floor-scale options — (a) the Stage 2B recipe, (b) Engram-scale memory midtraining, (c) flow unfreeze at scale — opened with formal mathematics, followed by creative alternatives, a literature check, and a plain-language summary. This is a decision brief, not a lock; nothing here authorizes work beyond the score-only probes flagged in §3.

## 1. Formal analysis

**1.1 The objective function, stated once.** Under the effect-floor doctrine the decision criterion is expected *claimable* effect per unit cost, subject to P(effect ≥ ~20 rows) being non-trivial. "Claimable" matters because of the reasoning-scope addendum: the 292-row teacher gap decomposes as GSM8K 104 + MMLU 80 + ARC-Easy 52 + ARC-Challenge 29 + MBPP 24 + Tier-1 3, of which the **primary claim batteries (GSM8K + MBPP) hold 128 rows**, ARC-Challenge 29 is secondary, and the remaining 135 rows (MMLU, ARC-Easy, Tier-1) are floor-only in this program era. An option whose natural gains land in floor-only batteries buys telemetry, not claims.

**1.2 Option (a): the depth-compounding model.** The current system is measured depth-invariant: one corrective shot whose capture ceiling is ~15 percent (π_dir, thrice replicated), converting to ~+5 rows. The Stage 2B hypothesis is that a depth-progressive objective makes the K loops *non-redundant corrective steps*. The idealized compounding bound: if each loop independently captures fraction ε of remaining error, K loops capture 1 − (1−ε)^K; at ε = 0.15, K = 4 gives **0.478 — a 3.2× multiplier over the one-shot ceiling**, mapping the measured +5-row conversion to ~+16, with the mHC carry and constitutive innovation existing precisely to raise per-loop ε (protected state, unbypassable recurrent path). Design range: **+10 to +25 rows, centered near the floor**. Two formal cautions bound the optimism. First, compounding requires *independence* of loop corrections — the whole point of the new objective, but an assumption until measured; correlated loops give much less than 1−(1−ε)^K. Second, an expressivity ceiling: the loops re-run the *frozen* middle block, so the reachable K-step trajectory family is parameterized only by the ~1.6M-parameter steering surface — h_{k+1} = R_frozen(B_θ(p, h_k)) is a fixed dynamical map steered through a 128-dim scratch channel with RMS-capped writes. Iterating a frozen map with low-dimensional steering is strictly weaker than iterating a trainable block (which is what the looped-LM literature trains); how much weaker is exactly what the M0–M6 curriculum's early phases measure cheaply before the main spend. Pro-side scorecard: targets the *primary* battery (GSM8K is the multi-step pocket, 104 gap rows — 36 percent of the whole gap, all claimable); attacks a *measured* failure (idle loops with moving state); design complete; cost ≈ one standard campaign. Con: the compounding assumption is unproven for frozen-substrate steering, and the P3.3 information ceiling may bind each loop identically (if every loop can only re-capture the *same* 15 percent, compounding collapses to ~ε and the campaign reads ~+5 again — this is the falsifier, and it is measurable at the first depth-curve look).

**1.3 Option (b): Engram-scale arithmetic.** The screen's values received ~10 gradient hits per slot from ~10^6–10^7 answer-masked supervised tokens; the regime where Engram demonstrates hybrid-memory gains is full-sequence midtraining at ≥10^9 tokens — **100–1000× our campaign compute**, weeks of A100-class time, plus the CV-1 lessons engineered in from day one (contrastive content-causality objective, answer-grade payloads, or fully learned values). The ceiling is the highest of the three — it addresses the knowledge mass — but the governance arithmetic cuts hard: the knowledge mass lives in MMLU + ARC-Easy = 132 **floor-only** rows. Under the current claim scope, option (b)'s natural gains are mostly unclaimable until Paper Three, where knowledge is in scope by design. Pro: highest raw ceiling, strongest external evidence at scale, and it is where the roadmap always placed this work. Con: 100–1000× cost, evidence transfers from 27B pretraining to 0.5B frozen-substrate sidecars unproven, content causality must be *built* (we now know it does not emerge from CE/KL co-adaptation), and the claimable yield this era is small.

**1.4 Option (c): flow unfreeze.** Adds ~380k flow parameters to the 1.2M surface (+32 percent) at 0.1× LR. The formal problem: the program *already ran* the discriminating experiment. i1 poured 99.998 percent of gradient into the aim path and moved +0.054pp — the plateau was information- and objective-limited, not parameter-limited — and the A_r audit shelved the capacity fork on measurement. Unfreezing flow adds parameters to a system whose binding constraint was never parameters. Standalone P(≥20 rows) is low; but as an **arm inside (a)** it becomes well-posed, because the depth-progressive objective changes what the flow is asked to do — the capacity question is worth re-asking only under an objective that could use capacity. Cost as an arm: ~0.3× additional. Verdict: never standalone; cheap and sensible as (a)'s capacity arm.

**1.5 Decision summary.** Rough utilities: (a) P(≥20 claimable rows) ≈ 0.3–0.4 at 1× cost, with the falsifier readable early; (b) ≈ 0.4–0.5 for *raw* effect at 10–100× cost but low claimable share this era; (c) ≈ 0.1 standalone at 0.3×. Expected claimable effect per unit cost orders the fork **(a) with (c) as an arm ≫ (b) now**, while (b) remains the Paper Three main event with a budget sized to its regime. The scope alignment is the quiet clincher: (a) spends its effect where the program's registered claims live.

## 2. Creative alternatives and force-multipliers (compose with any fork choice)

- **Lower the floor instead of only raising the effect: DEV-2 panel expansion.** The point-floor scales ~1/√N: a 2,048-row panel drops the ~1.95-point floor to ~1.4, a 3,072-row panel to ~1.1. We hold 9,207 labeled non-DEV rows; a fresh DEV-2 is nearly free to build, is uncontaminated for depth arms (memory arms would need a contamination audit), and permanently improves every future read. This is the cheapest legitimate sensitivity gain available and I recommend binding it into whichever campaign runs.
- **Seed-ensemble serving (score-only probe).** The double dissociation and 0.84–0.89 fix-set Jaccards imply ~10–15 non-shared fixes per seed. A margin-arbitrated two-sidecar deployment (one substrate, two 1.2M sidecars — parameter-trivial) could add ~+5–10 rows at **zero training cost**. A pre-registered arbitration rule (e.g., higher row-minimum margin wins) makes it deployment-legitimate rather than oracle selection. Worth one score-only session regardless of the fork; if it lands near +8–10, it stacks with (a)'s effect toward the floor.
- **Execution-verified depth targets.** The depth-progressive objective needs depth-k targets; for GSM8K/MBPP these can be *generated and execution-verified* (k-step partial solutions checked programmatically) rather than teacher-sampled — ground-truth-anchored curriculum, reusing the V(x) widening idea, immune to teacher ignorance on exactly the batteries (a) targets.
- **Margin-primary interim gates.** Inside any campaign, continuous margin lift is the sub-floor-sensitive progress signal; discrete rows are read only at endpoints. This converts the floor doctrine from a constraint into a design pattern: margins steer, rows decide.
- **Distillation-at-scale as the bridge to (b).** Full-sequence spec-dec/latent distillation (Stage 2C) raises supervised token counts 20–50× without new data, moving toward (b)'s regime on the reasoning axis first — a natural post-(a) step that keeps the Engram-scale bet where the budget exists.

## 3. Literature check

The looped/recurrent-depth literature has moved decisively in the direction of option (a)'s hypothesis — with one caveat that vindicates our own finding. Geiping et al.'s recurrent-depth model ("Huginn," NeurIPS 2025) shows a 3.5B model scaling *test-time* reasoning by iterating a latent block, with gains concentrated on math/reasoning benchmarks; the Ouro line ("Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models," arXiv 2510.25741) reports looped LMs matching much larger standard models on reasoning at 2–3× parameter efficiency; follow-ons ("Scaling test-time reasoning with recursive latent thoughts," arXiv 2510.07358; memory-efficient looped transformers, arXiv 2605.07721) extend the same picture. The caveat: **every positive result trains the loops with loop-aware objectives, most from pretraining** — none obtains depth gains from a depth-indifferent objective, which is precisely the idle-loop lesson we measured independently. The literature therefore supports (a)'s mechanism while confirming its necessary condition (the depth-progressive objective) and flagging its open risk (nobody has shown the effect through a *frozen* substrate with sidecar steering — that result would be novel, which is both the risk and the paper). On the memory side, the balance of evidence matches our scope analysis: retrieval/memory augmentation reliably helps knowledge-intensive tasks, and "Great Memory, Shallow Reasoning: Limits of kNN-LMs" (arXiv 2408.11815) finds explicitly that memory does *not* buy reasoning — i.e., option (b)'s gains land where our claim scope isn't, exactly as §1.3's decomposition predicts. Engram itself remains the strongest pro-(b) datum (hybrids beat pure sparsity at matched budget, with some reasoning transfer), but at a scale and training regime we cannot reach this era.

## 4. Plain-language summary

The math, the shelf of our own past measurements, and the literature all point the same way. Option (a) — retrain the system so each thinking loop must genuinely improve on the last, with the lane-based working memory and the no-bypass gate — is the only choice that aims at the batteries our claims are allowed to count, attacks a failure we actually measured, costs one normal campaign, and has a best case (each loop compounding the correction) that roughly triples the known one-shot ceiling, landing right at or above the new twenty-answer bar. Its honest risk is that the loops, steering a frozen model through a narrow channel, may each only be able to re-fix the *same* mistakes rather than new ones — and helpfully, that failure would be visible at the first checkpoint's depth curve, before most of the budget is spent. Option (b) — the full-scale memory build — has the biggest ceiling but costs a hundred to a thousand times more, its published successes come from a setting quite unlike ours, and most of its winnings would land in categories we've agreed not to claim until the next paper, which is exactly where the roadmap already schedules it. Option (c) — unfreezing more parameters — re-asks a question we already answered (parameters were never the bottleneck) and only makes sense as a cheap side-arm inside (a). Three free upgrades should ride along regardless: double the evaluation panel to lower the noise floor itself, test the two trained seeds as a margin-arbitrated ensemble (potentially five to ten answers for zero training), and build the depth curriculum's step-by-step targets from execution-checked math and code, so the teachers' blind spots can't cap it. My recommendation stands: **(a), with (c) as an arm, plus the panel expansion and the ensemble probe — and (b) stays the centerpiece of Paper Three with a budget sized to what it actually needs.**
