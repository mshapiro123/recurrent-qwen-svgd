## 2. The T1 formalism — seed-specific coordinates are a symmetry prediction, quantitatively confirmed

The headline T1 number deserves its formal frame, because it is not a disappointment — it is a confirmed prediction of a symmetry argument. The training objective is (approximately) invariant under orthogonal reparameterization of the scratch space: rotate the scratch basis by any W ∈ O(d) and counter-rotate the flow and bridge maps, and the system computes the same function. The objective therefore has a gauge freedom, independent runs select arbitrary gauges, and cross-seed raw coordinates should match at the level of *random* bases. The check: independent random unit vectors in R^128 have expected |cosine| = √(2/πd) = **0.0705**. The measured cross-seed core mean is **0.0715**. The match is essentially exact — the two seeds' coordinate systems are unrelated to one another at precisely the magnitude pure gauge freedom predicts (the measured distribution is tighter than literal randomness, hinting at a small shared component, worth one line in the paper, nothing more). Every consequence the agent drew is therefore endorsed: raw cross-instance cosine retrieval is the wrong instrument by construction, not by bad luck.

Two more T1 findings earn permanent status. **The depth trajectory is real and ordered** — monotone approach to the K=4 state in both seeds (0.89→1.00, 0.73→1.00). Combine this with the K+ result and the composite finding is sharper than either alone: in seed 0, the *state moves substantially with depth while the task output stays exactly flat* — depth computation exists and is then functionally neutralized by the readout, which is precisely what the §2 depth-invariance optimum looks like from inside the state space. The loops are not dead; they are idling. That is the cleanest motivation yet banked for the Stage 2B depth-progressive objective. And **within-seed lineage stability is total** (P3.4↔P3.5 core cosine 1.0000): the landing retrained the write machinery without moving state construction at all, which means fingerprints are checkpoint-stable within a lineage — exactly the property the Stage 2A spine needs.

The state-geometry study delivered the elegant result of the wave. The similarity between their alphabets is 0.0715, and pure mathematics says two *random* alphabets would score 0.0705: their coordinate systems are as different as chance allows, exactly as symmetry predicts, because nothing in training pins the alphabet down. So all future comparisons across models use translation-proof measurements, and any actual translation gets learned on one set of examples and tested on another. Best of all, combining the two waves: one model's internal state clearly moves as it thinks deeper while its answers don't change at all — the thinking machinery is running but idling, waiting for a training signal that makes depth pay. That signal is now the centerpiece of the phase after next. Nothing here delays the memory experiments already moving; it makes sure the big architectural choice is made on a measurement that means what it says.
