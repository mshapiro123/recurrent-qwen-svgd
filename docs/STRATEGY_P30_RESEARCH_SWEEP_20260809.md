Rule of engagement: findings change the program only through the P3.3/P3.4 locks, never mid-run. Bottom line first: **nothing in the literature contradicts the P3.3 pilot design — the lock can proceed unchanged.** The sweep yields one directly actionable new loss candidate for P3.4, two new named levers for P3.5, one principled justification for an existing design choice, and two cautions that go into the risk register with mitigations we mostly already have.

## 1. Direction-supervised interventions

**Findings.** Inference-Time Intervention [Li et al., 2023] showed that a small number of probe-derived directions, added to attention-head outputs at inference, materially shift model behavior (TruthfulQA 32.5%→65.1% on Alpaca) from a few hundred labeled examples — the probe-then-intervene pipeline is precedented, which is exactly the A6 linear-decodability-forecast → trained-bridge sequence. ReFT [Wu et al., 2024] trains low-rank interventions directly on hidden representations and beats LoRA on commonsense/arithmetic suites at 10–65× fewer parameters — strong evidence that *trained interventions on h are a competitive adaptation channel per parameter*, which is the sidecar's bet stated in someone else's receipts. The 2025–26 steering literature converges on two practitioner facts: static task-level steering vectors are effective for behavior control but unreliable for accuracy gains, and steering quality degrades as intervention norm grows — both of which favor our design (per-position, content-computed writes over static vectors; the bounded clamp over free norms).

**Effect on the program.** Confirms the P3.3 pilot design as-is. Adds one capacity lever for P3.5: if bridge capacity becomes the binding constraint, the LoReFT-style low-rank subspace intervention form (edit in a learned r-dim subspace of h rather than full-rank u) is the literature-backed shape for the upgrade.

## 2. Distillation for answer quality (future-token, hidden-state, on-policy)

**Findings.** Three results matter, one of them a real caution.

*On-policy versus off-policy.* MiniLLM [Gu et al., 2023] and GKD [Agarwal et al., 2023] establish the exposure-bias critique of teacher-forced forward-KL distillation and the case for student-generated sequences with mode-seeking (reverse) KL. The 2026 recipe study [Rethinking On-Policy Distillation, 2026] adds mechanism: successful distillation concentrates on high-probability overlap tokens (97–99% of combined mass — optimizing only those matches full-vocabulary training), needs an off-policy cold start when student and teacher thinking patterns diverge, and — the caution — finds that **same-family models of different sizes converge to similar distributions, so raw size gap does not guarantee transferable signal**, with a 1.5B teacher sometimes beating a 7B one.

*Small-student capacity mismatch.* The Small Model Learnability Gap [Li et al., 2025] is the sharpest external warning for this phase: sub-3B students *degrade* when distilled from large teachers' long chains of thought (Qwen2.5-0.5B lost >10 points on AMC moving from a 3B teacher's data to a 72B teacher's), with mix-distillation (0.2 large : 0.8 small) recovering 7–8 points. Mechanisms: distribution mismatch, missing domain knowledge, and style shift.

*Why our design partially dodges both.* Our distillation is token-level and latent-level at anchored positions, not long-CoT sequence SFT, so the style-shift mechanism is muted. The teachability stratum T(K) is precisely a learnability filter — concentrating loss where the gap is learnable is the mix-distillation insight implemented at position granularity. And the overlap-token finding retroactively justifies a design choice we made for storage reasons: the top-K = 128 lattice truncation is not an approximation to apologize for, it is where essentially all the transferable signal lives.

**Effect on the program.** P3.4 lock: keep forward-KL teacher-forced distillation for v1 (our supervised positions are anchored, the exposure-bias critique applies weakly, and the cold-start condition is satisfied by construction — the sidecar starts from the Phase 2 checkpoint). Two new P3.5 levers, entering the named-lever queue: **mix-teacher** (a Qwen2.5-3B lattice pass blended ≈0.8 small : 0.2 large, deployed if 14B-only distillation stalls — the learnability-gap remedy) and **on-policy pass** (augmented student generates, teacher scores, reverse KL, per the OPD recipe — deployed if teacher-forced gains plateau while battery gains lag). Risk register: the same-family caution is logged with its mitigation — our transfer signal is not assumed from size but measured (the battery-level 14B−base gap is the denominator of the currency, and the cascade's 16.748% disagreement rate is banked).

## 3. Self-correction training

**Findings.** SCoRe [Kumar et al., 2024] shows that supervised fine-tuning on self-correction traces collapses to no-edit or superficial-edit behavior, and that multi-turn self-correction only becomes real under two-stage RL with a reward that pays for *improvement between attempts* (+15.6% MATH self-correction over base). Intrinsic self-correction without training remains weak across the literature.

**Effect on the program.** The K-loop refinement is latent self-correction, and SCoRe names its failure mode: the flow can learn to do all its work in loop 1 and make later loops decorative identity maps — satisfying every loss while flattening the accuracy-versus-K curve that is the program's founding measurement. Deep supervision with w_k = k/K already pushes against this. The lock-level addition is telemetry, not loss: **the per-loop marginal-improvement curve (accuracy at k minus accuracy at k−1, per battery) is a standing chart in every P3.4 report-back**, and a flat curve triggers the SCoRe-style remedy as a named P3.5 lever — a progress term that rewards loop k only where it improves on loop k−1.

## 4. Latent lookahead and looped latent reasoning

**Findings.** Coconut [Hao et al., 2024] validates the premise (continuous latent thoughts can replace explicit chains, with breadth-first-like parallel exploration in latent space) but needs a staged curriculum and lags explicit CoT on some tasks. The recurrent-depth 3.5B model [Geiping et al., 2025] shows looped latent compute scaling test-time performance — the from-scratch cousin of Paper One's retrofit result. The most actionable is LOTUS [2026]: a looped transformer whose latent positions are supervised *directly through the frozen LM head* against gold reasoning tokens, in parallel, closing the latent-versus-explicit gap to within 1 point of CoT on GSM8K on a 3B model at 2.5–6.9× speedup — and its ablations show the parallel token-level supervision on latents is essential, not optional. Decoding latents through the head also recovered interpretable reasoning steps.

**Effect on the program.** This is the sweep's one directly actionable new loss candidate. Our scratchpad's four future slots are latent positions; the lattice's teacher tokens are their natural targets; the frozen base LM head (tied, zero new parameters) is the projection LOTUS uses. **Candidate loss for the P3.4 lock's optional arm: slot-level cross-entropy through the frozen head — project each future slot (through the existing canonicalizer inverse or a zero-init linear lift to d = 896), decode through the tied head, supervise against the lattice's teacher tokens at the corresponding horizon, deep-supervised across loops.** It is cheap (reuses the existing lattice and head), it grounds the scratchpad in token space without adding inference-time machinery, and it directly attacks the Attack-1 information question — if the slots can be trained to *name* the teacher's future tokens, the information the aim loss needs is demonstrably present in S. Recommended disposition: pre-registered optional arm in the P3.4 lock, evaluated as the arm's own gap_closed delta, and the P3.4 creativity slot is reserved for it if the lock's main arms are full. LOTUS's decode-the-latents trick also becomes free interpretability telemetry: a periodic table of what each slot decodes to, through the frozen head.

## 5. Consolidated deltas for the locks

**P3.3 lock (binding):** no changes from this sweep. The pilot's {L_aim, L_gate, annealed preserve} set, the probe-then-train sequence, and the bounded clamp all have direct literature support (ITI, ReFT, steering-norm findings).

**P3.4 lock (recommended):** forward-KL teacher-forced v1 retained with the justification now on record (anchored positions, cold start satisfied); top-K truncation recorded as principled per the overlap-mass finding; per-loop marginal-improvement telemetry mandatory; the LOTUS-style slot-CE arm pre-registered as optional.

**P3.5 named-lever queue (updated, in priority order):** oracle-direction refresh (unchanged, first), mix-teacher 3B lattice pass (new), on-policy distillation pass (new), flow unfreeze at 0.1×, slot-CE arm if not exercised in P3.4, loop-progress term if the K-curve is flat, bridge capacity via low-rank subspace intervention form, data expansion, per-loop gate schedules.

**Risk register additions:** same-family transfer caution (mitigated: gap measured at battery level, loss concentrated on measured-teachable positions); small-student capacity mismatch (mitigated: token-level transfer, teachability filter, mix-teacher lever staged); loop collapse (watched: marginal-improvement curve, remedy staged).
